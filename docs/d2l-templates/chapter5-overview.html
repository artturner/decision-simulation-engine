Chapter 5

Civil Rights

Overview

Civil rights require government to treat people equally under the law. This chapter explains the Equal Protection Clause and standards of judicial review, then follows struggles for equality involving African Americans, women, Indigenous peoples, and other groups whose rights have expanded through activism, legislation, and court decisions.

Learning goals

01

Explain how equal protection applies to discrimination and how courts review different classifications.

02

Trace how social movements, constitutional amendments, laws, and court cases expanded civil rights for historically excluded groups.

03

Assess the progress made toward equality and the legal, political, and social barriers that remain.

Goals at a glance

01 · Explain equal protection 02 · Trace major civil rights struggles 03 · Assess progress and remaining barriers

Why this matters

Civil rights affect who can vote, learn, work, marry, use public spaces, and receive equal treatment. This history shows that rights written on paper do not automatically become reality; people often have to organize, challenge institutions, and demand enforcement.
