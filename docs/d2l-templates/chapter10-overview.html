Chapter 10

Interest Groups and Lobbying

Overview

Interest groups organize people and resources to influence public policy without directly running the government. This chapter examines different kinds of groups, collective-action problems, lobbying strategies across all three branches, campaign spending, unequal access, and the debate over regulating political influence.

Learning goals

01

Distinguish types of interest groups and explain how incentives help groups overcome collective-action and free-rider problems.

02

Trace how interest groups use lobbying, information, litigation, grassroots activity, and campaign spending to influence government.

03

Evaluate whether interest-group activity improves participation or gives well-funded interests too much power, including the free-speech issues involved.

Goals at a glance

01 · Distinguish groups and incentives 02 · Trace pathways of influence 03 · Evaluate access, power, and regulation

Why this matters

Organized groups influence laws about education, business, health, rights, and nearly every other issue. Knowing how lobbying works helps you see who is trying to shape a decision, what resources they bring, and how joining with others can amplify your own voice.
