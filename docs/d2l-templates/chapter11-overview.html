Chapter 11

Congress

Overview

Congress represents the public, writes laws, controls spending, and checks the other branches. This chapter examines the design and powers of the House and Senate, congressional elections, models of representation, party and committee organization, and the formal and modern processes used to pass legislation.

Learning goals

01

Explain how the House and Senate differ and how Congress's constitutional powers support its role in government.

02

Analyze how elections, incumbency, district boundaries, and models of representation shape lawmakers' behavior.

03

Trace how leadership, committees, debate rules, and negotiations move a bill through the legislative process.

Goals at a glance

01 · Explain Congress's structure and powers 02 · Analyze elections and representation 03 · Trace how laws are made

Why this matters

Congress decides what becomes federal law and how public money is spent. Understanding its rules helps you follow a bill, interpret your representative's choices, and see why popular ideas may be changed, delayed, or defeated before becoming law.
