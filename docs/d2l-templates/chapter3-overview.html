Chapter 3

American Federalism

Overview

Federalism divides governing authority between the national and state governments. This chapter explains how the Constitution assigns powers, how federal-state relationships have changed, how grants and mandates connect levels of government, and why federalism creates both opportunities and problems.

Learning goals

01

Distinguish federal, unitary, and confederal systems and identify how the Constitution divides governmental powers.

02

Explain how American federalism has evolved and how grants, mandates, and court decisions shape intergovernmental relationships.

03

Assess the advantages and disadvantages of federalism in responding to public needs and regional differences.

Goals at a glance

01 · Distinguish systems and powers 02 · Explain changing federal-state relations 03 · Assess federalism's tradeoffs

Why this matters

Many policies that affect you — education, voting rules, health programs, roads, and public safety — involve more than one level of government. Federalism helps you identify who has authority, who pays, and whom to hold responsible when policies succeed or fail.
