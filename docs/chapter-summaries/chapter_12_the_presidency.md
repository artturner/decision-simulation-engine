# Chapter 12: The Presidency

## Chapter Overview

The presidency combines constitutional duties, expanded modern powers, and strong public expectations. This chapter explains the office's design and election, the growth of presidential authority, the executive branch's organization, the president's public leadership, and direct tools such as executive orders, agreements, and pardons.

## Learning Goals

1. **Trace** the design, election, and historical expansion of the presidency and presidential power.
2. **Explain** how presidents organize an administration, build a governing agenda, and use public communication to seek support.
3. **Assess** the uses and limits of direct presidential action within the constitutional system of checks and balances.

## Goals at a Glance

1. **Trace** presidential power and elections.
2. **Explain** presidential leadership.
3. **Assess** direct action and limits.

## Why This Matters

Presidents are highly visible, but they cannot govern alone. Knowing what the office can and cannot do helps you judge campaign promises, understand executive actions, and separate a president's real authority from the power people assume the president has.
