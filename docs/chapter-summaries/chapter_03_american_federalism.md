# Chapter 3: American Federalism

## Chapter Overview

Federalism divides governing authority between the national and state governments. This chapter explains how the Constitution assigns powers, how federal-state relationships have changed, how grants and mandates connect levels of government, and why federalism creates both opportunities and problems.

## Learning Goals

1. **Distinguish** federal, unitary, and confederal systems and identify how the Constitution divides governmental powers.
2. **Explain** how American federalism has evolved and how grants, mandates, and court decisions shape intergovernmental relationships.
3. **Assess** the advantages and disadvantages of federalism in responding to public needs and regional differences.

## Goals at a Glance

1. **Distinguish** systems and powers.
2. **Explain** changing federal-state relations.
3. **Assess** federalism's tradeoffs.

## Why This Matters

Many policies that affect you - education, voting rules, health programs, roads, and public safety - involve more than one level of government. Federalism helps you identify who has authority, who pays, and whom to hold responsible when policies succeed or fail.
