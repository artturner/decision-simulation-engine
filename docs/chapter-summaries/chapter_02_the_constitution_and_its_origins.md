# Chapter 2: The Constitution and Its Origins

## Chapter Overview

The U.S. Constitution grew out of colonial ideas about natural rights, self-government, and limited power. This chapter follows the break with Britain, the weaknesses of the Articles of Confederation, the compromises of the Constitutional Convention, the ratification debate, and the ways the Constitution can change.

## Learning Goals

1. **Trace** the political ideas and events that led from British rule to independence and the Articles of Confederation.
2. **Analyze** how constitutional compromises, federalism, separation of powers, and checks and balances shaped the new government.
3. **Evaluate** the arguments over ratification and the formal and informal ways the Constitution changes.

## Goals at a Glance

1. **Trace** the Constitution's origins.
2. **Analyze** its design and compromises.
3. **Evaluate** ratification and change.

## Why This Matters

The Constitution is the rulebook for American government, but it was built through conflict and compromise. Understanding why its rules exist makes it easier to judge today's disputes over rights, power, and whether the system should change.
