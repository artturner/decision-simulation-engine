# Chapter 15: The Bureaucracy

## Chapter Overview

The bureaucracy turns laws and presidential priorities into programs, rules, and public services. This chapter traces the shift from patronage to a merit-based civil service, compares bureaucratic models and agency types, and explains how elected officials, courts, citizens, and public employees seek accountability.

## Learning Goals

1. **Trace** the growth of public administration and the development of a merit-based federal civil service.
2. **Compare** major models of bureaucracy and the roles of departments, independent agencies, regulatory commissions, and government corporations.
3. **Evaluate** how rulemaking, oversight, transparency laws, and whistleblower protections control bureaucratic power.

## Goals at a Glance

1. **Trace** bureaucracy and civil service.
2. **Compare** models and agency types.
3. **Evaluate** control and accountability.

## Why This Matters

Congress may pass a law, but agencies usually decide how it works in practice. Bureaucratic rules affect food safety, student aid, transportation, workplaces, and the environment, so understanding agencies helps you see where policy becomes real and how the public can demand accountability.
