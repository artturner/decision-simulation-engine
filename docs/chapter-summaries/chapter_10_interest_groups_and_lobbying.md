# Chapter 10: Interest Groups and Lobbying

## Chapter Overview

Interest groups organize people and resources to influence public policy without directly running the government. This chapter examines different kinds of groups, collective-action problems, lobbying strategies across all three branches, campaign spending, unequal access, and the debate over regulating political influence.

## Learning Goals

1. **Distinguish** types of interest groups and explain how incentives help groups overcome collective-action and free-rider problems.
2. **Trace** how interest groups use lobbying, information, litigation, grassroots activity, and campaign spending to influence government.
3. **Evaluate** whether interest-group activity improves participation or gives well-funded interests too much power, including the free-speech issues involved.

## Goals at a Glance

1. **Distinguish** groups and incentives.
2. **Trace** pathways of influence.
3. **Evaluate** access, power, and regulation.

## Why This Matters

Organized groups influence laws about education, business, health, rights, and nearly every other issue. Knowing how lobbying works helps you see who is trying to shape a decision, what resources they bring, and how joining with others can amplify your own voice.
